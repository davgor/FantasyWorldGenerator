# LAB-017 — scoped adversarial review

Compared the city-layout implementation with `docs/terrain-math-lab.md` and the ticket's deterministic, terrain-safe acceptance rules.

## Finding and resolution

The initial candidate search preserved slope and river-buffer limits only on its first pass. When no candidate existed, a fallback search accepted any land node and the layout builder could substitute the city node itself. That contradicted the documented constraints while presenting the resulting anchors as compliant. The fallback relaxation and implicit city-node substitution were removed. Plans now keep their target counts, emit a `fallback` explanation, and leave unavailable anchor lists short or empty.

Anchor assignment also advanced its cursor while applying an offset, which could skip unused candidates and report an avoidable shortfall. Assignment now rotates the deterministic candidate list once and consumes it sequentially, preserving uniqueness without skipping valid nodes.

## Evidence and limits

Focused regression tests exercise the rejected fallback and generate the same world twice to compare all settlement output. Every generated anchor is checked against water, slope and computed river distance, and every city exposes the expected seven feature groups with pack/profile metadata. Full repository gates are recorded in the consolidation handoff.

This review establishes standalone planning data only. It does not establish UE5 building placement, streets, navigation, bridge geometry, city quality, or runtime performance.
