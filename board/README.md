**2026-09-21 ruling — an age is five thousand years, and a quest does not cross one.** Recorded at [028](../docs/decisions/028-an-age-is-five-thousand-years.md) and landed the same day. `AGE_YEARS` was `100.`, and its own comment admitted the number was invented because a tick could not route a long span without one. It is now `5000.`, so a century is a `LONG` tick rather than an age advance and a five-thousand-year span is one age advance rather than fifty. The load-bearing half is easy to miss: the ley per-year bounds are the age bounds taken to `1/AGE_YEARS`, and because `terrain_time.py` *asks* the constant rather than restating its consequence, the exponent moved on its own — the literal `0.65 ** 0.01` lived only in prose. Had it been written out, one age of ordinary ticking would have composed to `0.65 ** 50` ≈ 2e-10, draining the magic layer to zero with every individual step inside its stated bounds and no test failing. Measured after: 0.650000000000018 and 1.349999999999356 over one age; with the exponent forced back, 4.4225e-10 and 3,286,157.88. Quests are now reaped at an age turn under a distinct `age_turned` reason and the board rebuilt from the new age's hooks, which retires the ten incidental survivors — they lived only because the regenerated cast re-minted positional uids as the same string, and each carried the old age's prose against the new age's target check with a `giver_uid` naming nobody. `POST /world/advance-age` does **not** reap, because the reap needs a day and that route has none, so a caller turning ages through it directly still carries the chimera; stated in the record rather than fixed.
