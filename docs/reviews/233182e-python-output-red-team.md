# Python output red team — commit `233182e`

Persona: game developer, reading the emitted world as shippable content. Reviewed 2026-09-20
against `233182e` ("next batch"). Scope set by the coordinator session: **the data the Python
generator emits, judged as game content** — not whether the code is correct, not the C++ module.
No product code was changed; nothing under `Sim/`, `Core/`, `Contracts/` or `tools/` was written.

Verdict: **the world's wiring is sound and its content is thin, uniform, and in two places
reports its own failure.** Every cross-block reference I could find resolves — 100% of NPC
building placements, hero claims, nomad `basis` fields and key-location provenance land on a
record that exists. That is a genuinely strong result and it is not what I expected to find. The
problems are one level up: a reference world contains **seven places worth entering** and 1,072
people who can offer to send you to one; danger is distributed with no relationship to distance
from safety; the index a quest handler is meant to query for "what is near here" returns
sharks; and the record nested inside every settlement says the city has no buildings while the
planner two blocks away placed 133 of them.

### The reference world has almost no terrain, and it qualifies some of this

Verified in both documents after the coordinator flagged it. `resolved_octaves` is **0 of 5 at
size 17 and 1 of 5 at size 33**, against `effective_config.octaves: 5`, and the generator says so
in its own warnings: *"Only 0/5 surface-noise octaves resolved; fine layers omitted."* Until the
grid ceiling moved to 1025, a world could have all its terrain or all its history and never both
— `terrain_history.py` refused age advancement above 257, while the octave filter admits four of
five octaves only at 257 and all five at 513. **Every measurement in this review is on a world
with essentially no surface relief.** Settlement `slope_degrees` medians are 5.6° and 5.0°.

Being explicit about what that does and does not touch, because the honest answer differs by
finding:

**Unaffected.** The gradient (findings 2, 2b) — nests and settlements sit on the same smooth
terrain, so adding relief moves both and the statistic is a relationship between them. The
content budget ratio (8), the `city_layout` contradiction (1), the `(Age N)` names (4), the two
empty systems (7), the population triple-count (6), the catalogue split (10) and the missing
hook verb and difficulty (8) are all counts, contradictions or schema shapes that do not read the
heightfield.

**Affected — but only one of the two placement blocks, and the difference is in the code.** My
first draft of this section hedged both `key_locations` and `beast_nests`. That was too broad;
the gamer-persona session objected on `key_locations` and was right, and checking the objection
turned up the sharper fact:

- **`key_locations` gates on percentiles, so it is octave-invariant.**
  `Sim/key_locations/__init__.py:47` states it — *"Requirements are percentile-relative to each
  world['s] own distribution"* — and `catalogue.py:20` carries `above_percentile` /
  `below_percentile` term keys. A zero-octave field still has a median, so the thresholds still
  bite. **Its counts are not an octave artefact and my hedge on finding 8 was wrong.**
- **`beast_nests` gates on absolute field values, so it is octave-sensitive.**
  `terrain_nests.py:84` is `if any(f.get(k,0) < v for k,v in p['requires'].items())`, and the
  score on the next line is `clamp(f.get(k,0)) * w / total` — raw field magnitudes throughout, no
  percentile anywhere.

#### Scope correction: relief starvation explains 11% of the catalogue, not the catalogue

An earlier revision said the zero-octave terrain "may be the real reason 267 of 358 species never
place." **That was too broad and the source refutes it.** Tallying what the 369 monster profiles
actually gate on:

- **Hard gates (`requires`)** are overwhelmingly magical: `ley_umbral` 75 profiles, `ley_weave`
  71, `ley_primordial` 70, `ley_holy` 34, `ley_infernal` 33, `wetland` 28. Relief fields are a
  tail — `mountain` 9, `volcanic` 6, `depth` 5.
- **Score terms (`weights`)** are led by `moisture` (220 profiles) and the same ley fields. Only
  three weight fields are relief-derived at all: `mountain`, `volcanic`, `depth`.
- **42 of 369 monsters (11%) carry any relief-derived weight; 22 (6%) draw half or more of their
  score from one.** The other 327 gate on magic, moisture and wetland, none of which are surface
  noise.

So most of the catalogue's non-placement is not a terrain story. **But for the 11% that is, the
effect is severe and measurable** — placement rates by relief dependence, across the two rasters:

| profile group | n | placed at size 17 (0 octaves) | placed at size 33 (1 octave) |
|---|---|---|---|
| relief-dominant (≥50% of score) | 22 | 2 (9.1%) | 12 (**54.5%**) |
| relief-touched (<50%, >0) | 20 | 0 (0.0%) | 3 (15.0%) |
| no relief term | 327 | 74 (22.6%) | 131 (40.1%) |

**One octave raised relief-dominant placement six-fold while the relief-free majority improved
1.8×.** That is direct evidence that octave admission gates this group specifically, which is
what the hedge got right.

#### The 513 run is now a binary test, not an investigation

`brimstone-bats` sits in the 6%: hard gate magical (`ley_infernal >= 0.025`), score
`{mountain: 2, volcanic: 2, ley_infernal: 2}` — two thirds relief. It places **zero at both 0 and
1 octave**. So does **every one of the six species requiring `volcanic`**, at both rasters, while
`requires: mountain` went 1 of 9 to 6 of 9 with the first octave.

That splits the question cleanly, and either outcome is informative:

- **If relief starvation is the cause**, five octaves at size 513 should lift the relief-dominant
  group further and start placing the `volcanic`-gated six, `brimstone-bats` among them.
  `BESTIARY-BRIMSTONE-BATS` is then a terrain defect and the catalogue needs no retune.
- **If the `volcanic` field is itself degenerate** — never reaching any profile's threshold
  regardless of resolution — the six stay at zero while `mountain`-gated species keep improving.
  That is a sharper defect than the card currently describes, and it is in the field rather than
  in the creature.

`mountain` already separating from `volcanic` at one octave is weak evidence for the second. The
measurement is twenty lines against the document and the script is written.

#### Four causes wearing one symptom — and my "degenerate field" hypothesis was wrong

I proposed that the `volcanic` field is degenerate and that two key-location archetypes plus six
creature profiles failing identically pointed at the field. **That is refuted, by the coordinator
and the gamer-persona session independently, and they were right: I reasoned from gate
definitions and never read the field values.** Reading them separates four distinct causes that
had been wearing one symptom. Only the first is mine and only the first is new.

**1. Domain/field mismatch — resolution-independent, and nothing else explains it.**
Three archetypes declare `domain: land` and gate on a field that is **identically zero on land**:

| archetype | gate | land cells ≥ floor | water cells ≥ floor | land max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `salt_mine` | `salinity ≥ 0.02` | 0 of 50 | 239 of 239 | **0.0000** |
| `salt_pans` | `salinity ≥ 0.02` | 0 of 50 | 239 of 239 | **0.0000** |
| `whaling_station` | `fishing_productivity ≥ 0.02` | 0 of 50 | 239 of 239 | **0.0000** |

At size 17 these fields are nonzero on every water cell and zero on every land cell. At size 33
they reach 2 of 279 land cells, which are coastal straddles. **Raising the raster does not put
ocean salinity inland**, so unlike everything else in this section these do not recover — they
report `candidates: 0`, *"no ground in this world satisfies the requirements"*, and that will be
true at 1025. A land-domain archetype gated on an ocean-only field is unplaceable by
construction.

**2. Tail calibration — the gamer-persona session's diagnosis, and the correct one for
`volcanic`.** The field fires; its useful range is a sliver. Roughly half of cells are nonzero,
the median is 0.0000 and the 90th percentile 0.0067, while every gate in either package sits at
0.04–0.08 — the 98th to 99.9th percentile of a field whose maximum is 0.05 to 0.15. Decisively,
**`volcanic` on land** has max **0.0286 at size 17**, below `geyser_basin`'s 0.05 floor, and
**0.1524 at size 33**, above it. That is exactly why the diagnostics change:

```
size 17   geyser_basin  wanted 2  candidates 0   "no ground in this world satisfies the requirements"
          lava_tube     wanted 1  candidates 0   same
size 33   geyser_basin  wanted 3  candidates 8   "spacing and clearance left room for fewer"
          lava_tube     wanted 1  candidates 3   same
```

**The archetypes change failure mode between rasters.** They are not a second consumer of a dead
field, and they are not evidence for my hypothesis. `volcanic` is not in class 1: it is nonzero
on 25 of 50 land cells at size 17 and 129 of 279 at size 33.

**3. Spacing squeeze.** The dominant mode across the whole catalogue — 60 of 98 archetypes at
size 17, 36 at size 33 — and what kills `geyser_basin` and `lava_tube` once the ground exists.
Orthogonal to resolution in the sense that raising it does not remove the squeeze.

**4. Area-scaled rounding.** The wonders, and in the creature package `phoenixes`, which gates on
`volcanic ≥ 0.04` alone with no ley conjunct and still places zero where cells clear it.

**What this does to the 513 run.** It is no longer a decisive test of anything, which is the
honest correction. Expect **partial** recovery: the field's land maximum rises with octaves, so
some of class 2 recovers, class 1 recovers never, and classes 3 and 4 are untouched by
resolution. It remains worth running for bestiary coverage generally, and the prediction to hold
it to is "partial, concentrated in the relief-dominant group", not "they all place" or "they all
stay dead".

`BESTIARY-BRIMSTONE-BATS` sits in class 2 with a ley conjunct, not in class 1.

#### The `key_locations` gate tally, and what it does to the wonder conclusion

97 archetypes: **69 percentile-only, 20 carrying both, 7 with no numeric terms, 1 absolute-only**
(`border_fort`). So 21 of 97 carry an absolute floor or ceiling.

It matters because of how the two combine, at `Sim/key_locations/core/placement.py:110-115`:

```python
cut = percentile(values, term['above_percentile'])
rule['min'] = max(cut, term['min']) if 'min' in term else cut
```

**The stricter of the two wins.** On a low-magnitude world the percentile cut falls below the
absolute floor and the floor dominates — so percentile-invariance does not protect those 21, and
the blanket claim that `key_locations` is octave-invariant is too strong.

**The wonders are not among them.** `maelstrom`, `titan_skeleton`, `fallen_star`, `great_stump`,
`the_scar`, `drowned_temple`, `library_ruin` and `colossus` are all pure percentile, so their
`wanted: 0` against 24–289 qualifying candidates is the area-scaled rounding the gamer-persona
session diagnosed, not a floor. The conclusion that raising the raster will not by itself produce
a wonder stands — it needs "for the wonders, which are percentile-only" attached rather than
being stated of the block.

The 21 floors cluster on the fields a degenerate world suppresses: `metal_richness` 6,
`coastal_exposure` 5, `temperature` 3, `salinity` 2, `frontier` 2, `volcanic` 2.

#### The animal catalogue is less relief-exposed than the monster one

311 animal profiles: 22 relief-dominant, 2 relief-touched — **24 of 311 (7.7%)**, against
monsters' 42 of 369 (11.4%). Animal hard gates are `wetland` 45, with `mountain` 2 and `depth` 3;
score terms are led by `moisture` 114 and `wetland` 54, and exactly one animal profile carries a
`volcanic` weight. The animal layer is a moisture-and-wetland system almost end to end, so the
513 run informs the monster half of the world and barely touches the half that reaches the
player.

**What still holds regardless:** retuning the catalogue against a zero-octave heightfield would
bake the degeneracy in, and that is the mistake worth preventing. It is just a mistake available
for 42 profiles, not 267.

`beast_movements` stranding (5) routes over the same terrain and carries the same caveat; I have
added it to that card.

**The highest-value follow-up in this review is therefore not mine:** a generation at size 513
with all five octaves resolved would settle whether the bestiary or the terrain is at fault, and
nobody has run it. It is a different question from the seed-73 run — seed 73 asks whether the
placement rule *can* produce a gradient, which is the defect question; size 513 asks whether the
catalogue is broken or merely starved, which is the retuning question.

## Findings

### 1. Every city reports that it has no buildings, beside a planner that built them

`settlements.sites[].city_layout.buildings.options` reports, for **all nine cities**,
`placed_count: 0` against targets of 43 to 68, three or four of them flagged `required`, and a
`fallback.reason` of *"Not enough valid non-water land anchors for requested feature and building
placement counts within local search band."*

`city_plans.cities[]`, for the same nine cities, holds **82 to 137 placed plots** each, with
building ids, metre coordinates, rotations, footprints, road access, worker and bed counts.

Both are in the same document. The failing one is nested inside the settlement record — the
first place anyone reading a settlement looks — and the working one is a sibling top-level block.
A consumer that trusts the obvious field concludes this world has no architecture.

Card: `CONTENT-CITY-LAYOUT-DEAD-BLOCK`.

### 2. There is no difficulty gradient anywhere in the world

Distance from each beast nest to the nearest city, grouped by the nest's danger tier:

| tier | n | median | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 63 | 13.5 km | 6.2 | 37.1 |
| 2 | 59 | 16.1 km | 6.2 | 37.1 |
| 3 | 24 | 13.2 km | 6.3 | 25.7 |
| 4 | 61 | 13.6 km | 4.8 | 37.1 |
| 5 | 94 | 13.8 km | 4.8 | 37.1 |

Five medians inside a 2.9 km band, and the deadliest tier reaches closer to a city (4.8 km) than
the safest does (6.2 km). Key locations are the same story against their own `threat` axis:
threat 1 sits a median 4.5 km out, threat 5 a median 7.4 km, and the ranges overlap almost
entirely.

A player leaving any gate in this world meets tier 1 and tier 5 with equal probability. Nothing
in the emitted data lets a game build a starting region, and no amount of downstream tuning
recovers a gradient that was never placed.

The gamer-persona session measured the same claim a second way and got the same answer: the
share of tier-4-and-5 nests is 50.0% within 10 km of a settlement, 51.6% within 25 km, 51.7%
within 50 km and 51.5% world-wide — flat to 1.7 percentage points. Two independent statistics
over the same data; both are worth keeping.

This is spatial, and distinct from `BESTIARY-PYRAMID-RETUNE`, which is about the *counts* being
inverted (94 tier-5 against 63 tier-1 here, consistent with that card's diagnosis). Fixing the
pyramid does not produce a gradient; they are two changes.

Card: `CONTENT-NO-DIFFICULTY-GRADIENT`.

### 2b. The land is empty because the monster budget is spent at sea

Found by the gamer-persona session and verified here independently, because it is the mechanism
behind both finding 2's tier skew and the catalogue coverage that
`PRODUCT-REACHABILITY-REPORT` tracks.

`beast_nests.sites[].kind` over 301 sites: **`aquatic territory` 232 (77%)**, `lair` 56, `roost`
8, `colony` 5. The six most common species are bladefish (28), sea serpents (18), shipbreaker
crabs (18), leviathan (16), kraken (15) and abyssal krakens (12). The world is 80.5% ocean, the
placer is a thinned point process over suitable ground, and the ocean is suitable ground for a
large part of the catalogue — so the budget goes where the area is.

**That leaves 69 monster territories on 2,487 km² of land: one per 36 km².** It also explains
which species never place. The genre's starter monsters — zombies, skeletons, skeletal hounds,
grave mites, corpse moths — are land monsters, and the land is a fifth of the world; the aquatic
catalogue is top-heavy with leviathans and krakens. The tier skew and the empty land are one
fact seen from the catalogue end and the map end.

Removing the aquatic sites does not recover a gradient either: the 69 land nests are tiered
13 / 9 / 13 / 15 / 19 from tier 1 to tier 5, still inverted.

### 2c. `tier` and `threat` point opposite ways at both extremes

Cross-tabulating the two integers that sit side by side in every `key_locations` record —
`tier` 0–3 (physical scale) against `threat` 1–5 (danger):

| tier \ threat | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 17 | 11 | 4 | 2 | **4** |
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | **3** | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 |

The four tier-0 sites at threat 5 are roadside waystones — *"the fifth stone on the Gulddum
(Age 1) road"*, `occupant: none`, `state: abandoned`. A milestone with nobody at it is rated
maximum danger. The three tier-2 sites at threat 1 are a march fort, a monastery and a crystal
cavern, all `occupant: none`.

The rubric is doing what it says — threat is derived from occupant class, state, weave
instability and nearby nest tier, and a waystone standing in a bad neighbourhood inherits the
neighbourhood. But the emitted result is that the most dangerous-looking numbers in the block sit
on its least substantial objects. Any consumer filtering for danger gets four road markers first.

The naming half of this belongs to `PRODUCT-CONSUMER-VOCABULARY` and the render half to the
gamer-persona session; recorded here because the data is worse than the naming collision alone
suggests.

### 3. `encounters` — the designated "what is near here" index — is mostly ocean fish

`encounters` is the block a quest handler is meant to query, and its own `method` says so:
indexed *"by month and by terrain node so a consumer answers 'what is near here'"*. It holds 805
entries across 159 nodes.

- **792 of 805 come from `beast_movements`; 13 from `nomads`.**
- The seven most common kinds are narwhal (89), Greenland shark (75), Atlantic bluefin tuna (69),
  blue shark (69), shortfin mako (69), swordfish (69) and oceanic whitetip (43) — **483 entries,
  60% of the index, are pelagic fish**, on a world that is 80.5% ocean.
- **790 of 805 entries are `disposition: wary`.** Six are hostile, five fleeing, four devout.
- `threat_tier` tops out at **4** (13 entries), although the world holds 94 tier-5 nests.

The block is honest about itself — its `limits` reads *"An index, not a quest generator: no
objectives, stakes, rewards or difficulty, because the consumer that would define them does not
exist yet"* — and the indexing work is real. But as the access path for encounter content it
currently answers "what is near here" with a shark a player cannot reach, and it is the only
block that carries a difficulty number at all.

Card: `CONTENT-ENCOUNTERS-ARE-FISH`.

### 4. The `(Age N)` build annotation is player-facing text

Heritage's morphemic namer has landed and it is good: cities are `Durlbral`, `Froskor`,
`Nordbrig`, `Wenbrig`, `Halbrig`; ruins are `Frosnord`, `Taelaeril`, `Thaltaur`; people are
`Torang`, `Gundsirn`, `Mokhgund`. In `snap-42-17`, three hours earlier, the same nine cities were
`Cedar City`, `Ember City`, `Glen City`, `Hazel City` and `Ivy City`. That is a large improvement
inside one evening.

What did not move is the disambiguator. Four of nine live cities and four of fourteen ruins carry
a parenthetical age: `Bargdum (Age 1)`, `Graflin (Age 2)`, `Guntmek (Age 2)`, `Pikdum (Age 2)`.
It then propagates into every derived string — **18 of 48 key-location names** carry it, including
mid-phrase:

> `the fourth stone on the Gulddum (Age 1) road`
> `the bright hollow under Ongbod (Age 1)`

A generation-pass ordinal is not a thing a world contains. This is the cheapest finding in the
review to fix and the most visible one if it ships.

Card: `CONTENT-AGE-SUFFIX-IN-NAMES`.

### 5. One third of the travelling world never moves

`beast_movements` considers 792 groups, **routes 532 and strands 260** — 33%. A stranded group
carries no `legs`, so it is a herd that exists at a point and never walks the seasonal round the
block was built to produce. The block reports the number plainly (`"stranded": 260`), which is
how I found it; nothing acts on it.

Card: `CONTENT-BEAST-MOVEMENTS-STRANDED`.

### 6. A city has three different populations and they differ by two orders of magnitude

For `Froskor`, the largest city:

| source | value |
|---|---|
| `settlements.sites[].population_estimate` | 28,306 |
| `settlements.sites[].urban_population_estimate` | 15,568 |
| beds across its `city_plans` plots | 244 |
| people the `npcs` roster places there | 247 |

The roster tracks the beds, which is the coherent choice and means the two blocks a game would
actually instantiate agree with each other. Nothing reconciles the demographic estimate, and it
is the number a settlement record leads with. A town sign reading "pop. 28,306" over 133
buildings and 247 residents is a promise the world cannot keep.

Fortresses are a sharper version: all four castle plans report **0 beds** and 37–42 `workers`,
and the roster places exactly that many people in each. Every garrison in the world sleeps
nowhere, and `workers` is silently doing duty as "people present".

Card: `CONTENT-POPULATION-THREE-NUMBERS`.

### 7. Two systems are wired, documented, and emit nothing

- **`heroes.dreads: []`.** 131 hero candidates are rolled and 67 precipitate into 60 people
  across ten roles — council, heir, domain holder, keeper, warlord, sovereign, castellan and
  others. The antagonist role fires zero times. `story_web` then weaves 54 of those 60 into 51
  reachable tropes including `the_siege` (6 offers), `revenge` (2) and `the_blight` (7) — a cast
  with motives, beliefs and claims, and no one to oppose.
- **`magic.colleges: []` against `effective_config.college_count: 2`.** Magic is enabled, twelve
  schools resolve, 78 ley nodes are placed and fourteen of them are key points raised by
  destroyed cities. The institutions that would let a player engage with any of it are absent.
  The block's `college_method` says *"Sites may be fewer than requested"*, which covers one of
  two; it reads differently at zero.

Both may be size-17 clipping. Neither is reported as a shortfall anywhere a consumer would see:
`heroes.summary.dreads` is `0` with no reason attached, and `magic` emits no diagnostic for the
colleges it did not place. `key_locations` already does exactly this — every archetype appears in
`sites` or in `diagnostics` with a reason — which is the pattern `PRODUCT-REACHABILITY-REPORT`
asks to generalise, and these are two more instances for that card.

Card: `CONTENT-NO-ANTAGONIST`.

### 8. The content budget: 1,072 givers, 7 destinations

This is the product bet, stated as a ratio.

**Supply.** `npcs` holds 2,296 people. 1,822 (79.4%) sit on a post carrying at least one of the
ten canonical verbs. 1,072 (46.7%) sit on a post carrying an *adventure* verb — slay, reclaim,
cleanse, recover, defend or investigate. Per verb: supply 708 people, defend 610, investigate
506, trade 480, tend 269, slay 234, cleanse 233, recover 65, convert 45, reclaim 40.

**Demand.** `key_locations` holds 48 sites. **38 of them are tier 0** and all 38 are
`placement: path` — they are the members of five waystone chains along roads. Three are tier 1.
**Seven are tier 2**, the interactable set. 27 of 48 have `occupant: none` and 30 are
`abandoned`. Alongside them: 14 ruins, 13 religion sites, 7 hero camps, 13 nomad bands, 0
colleges.

The count of key locations rose from 10 to 48 between my two documents. **That increase is 38
roadside waystones**; the interactable set went from 4 to 7. The composition pass did what it
said — chains in walk order, with a real sightline check behind the beacon lines — and it added
set dressing, not destinations.

So: **1,072 people who can offer to send a player somewhere dangerous, and seven places to send
them.** 153 to 1. On 2,487 km² of land that is one tier-2 location per 355 km².

I do not think the fix is more locations. `heroes.quest_hooks` is the interesting half and it is
already good: 49 hooks, each with a `giver_uid` that resolves to a named hero, a
`stated_purpose` in plain English, an `actual_effect` with a mechanical delta, and an
`offered_from_face` distinguishing the public ask from the private one —

> "Weaken the umbral node umbral-node-6; it chokes the radiant."
> `{"kind": "ley_node", "node_id": "umbral-node-6", "school": "umbral", "intensity_delta": -0.5}`

Two things hold it back. **No hook carries a verb or a difficulty** — the record is
`{hook_id, giver_uid, offered_from_face, stated_purpose, actual_effect, unwitting, target}` — so
nothing joins a hook to `key_locations.threat` or `beast_nests.tier`. And **ten of the 49 target
a ley node**, which is a direction vector and an intensity inside a per-school network: there is
no asset, no footprint and nothing for a player to stand in front of. `unwitting` is `false` on
all 49, so the betrayal mechanic the field exists for never fires in this world.

### 9. What is actually good, stated plainly

A red team that only reports defects gives a false picture of this output.

- **Referential integrity is complete.** 2,236 NPC building placements resolve to a real plot in
  the right plan; 60 hero claims all resolve; every nomad `basis` field resolves, including both
  `ley_node_id`s into the correct school network; 54 story webs resolve to heroes; 60 cast NPCs
  resolve to heroes. I found no dangling reference anywhere in the document.
- **`key_location_plans` closes the interior gap.** 48 plans, 182 plots, 3.79 parts per plan,
  each in a local metre frame with its own 17×17 heightfield and a kit resolved per archetype.
  Small, but it is geometry where the dossier expected a graph.
- **`wildlife` is the most complete block in the world**: 4,065 sites, every one `real: true`,
  with dens, roles and a thinned point process whose density falls with tier. Against it, all 301
  `beast_nests` sites are `real: false` and the block says so (*"a claim on ground, not a
  simulated creature"*). The world instantiates its animals and proposes its monsters. That
  asymmetry is worth a deliberate decision rather than inheriting it. **See finding 10 before
  reading this as unqualified praise** — the reason `wildlife` is complete is not entirely a
  compliment.
- **`story_web` and `heroes.persona`** are the strongest authored content here — beliefs, wants,
  fears, tells, alignment drift, 51 reachable tropes with a sane spread. If a game ships from
  this generator, this is the block it ships on.
- **`settlement_candidates`** records refugee bands that settled, with the reason: *"Refugees from
  surface-city-0-147-elf who reached surface-city-0-100-human_heartland and stayed."* That is a
  quest premise emitted for free.

### 10. The creature catalogue holds two authored populations, and only one of them is fantasy

Found by the gamer-persona session in `encounters`; extended and then narrowed here, because the
first two explanations offered for it were both wrong and the true one is more interesting.

`Sim/icarus_sim/terrain_nest_profiles.json` holds **680 profiles in one file**, and they split
perfectly on `class`:

| class | n | `real` | ids carrying an Earth toponym |
|---|---|---|---|
| monster | 369 | `false` on all 369 | **0 (0.0%)** |
| animal | 311 | `true` on all 311 | **44 (14.1%)** |

`african-buffalo`, `african-elephant`, `american-alligator`, `american-pika`, `arctic-fox`,
`arctic-hare`, `atlantic-cod`, `eurasian-lynx`, `central-american-agouti`. In the emitted world
that becomes **36 species across 873 of 4,065 `wildlife` sites — 21.5%** — and **zero** in
`beast_nests`. Seven of them surface in `encounters` across 175 of its 805 entries, so the block
a quest handler queries for "what is near here" answers *Atlantic bluefin tuna* on a world with
leylines, hidden gods and twelve schools of magic.

**Two explanations were proposed and both are false; recording them because acting on either
would have been wrong.** It is not confined to `wildlife` — the toponyms are in the shared file.
And it is *not* a silent filter excluding a third of a shared catalogue, which was the sharper
hypothesis: the cross-tab above shows the exclusion is exact and total on `class`, so nothing is
being dropped. Both readings came from reasoning over the corpus rather than splitting it; the
count that produced the filter hypothesis appears to have double-counted, treating each profile's
`id` and `name` as two entries.

**What is actually true is a product question, not a bug.** One file holds a fantasy bestiary of
369 invented monsters that are never instantiated, and a terrestrial fauna database of 311 real
species that always are. `real` tracks the split exactly. That reads like a simulation substrate
and a content catalogue sharing a table — which is defensible — except that the substrate is the
half reaching the player, through `encounters` and through 21.5% of every animal a world contains.

No rename table fixes it. The choice is a fantasy naming pass over the animal half, or an explicit
decision that this world's fauna is Earth's and the leylines are laid over a familiar planet. That
is a decision someone should make on purpose. The gamer-persona session owns the player-facing
half as `PLAYER-ATLANTIC-BLUEFIN`.

### 11. Resolution is the content budget, and it moves the story

The size-33 document (same seed, same 200 km planet) against size 17:

| | size 17 | size 33 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| land km² | 2,487 | 3,665 | 1.47× |
| cities | 9 | 40 | 4.4× |
| hamlets | 13 | 108 | 8.3× |
| key locations | 48 | 247 | 5.1× |
| nests | 301 | 1,023 | 3.4× |
| npcs | 2,296 | 9,566 | 4.2× |

Counts relayed from the performance and gamer-persona sessions; the nest, key-location and
settlement figures I re-measured myself. **Land area grows 47% on an identical planet** — the
coastline is a property of how finely it was sampled — and settlements grow roughly eleven times
faster than the land they stand on, from one per 95.6 km² to one per 17.3 km².

Two consequences for anyone treating `size` as a quality setting:

- **The interactable set scales but its composition does not — and the filler share moves in
  opposite directions along the two axes.** `key_locations.kind` is the archetype field, distinct
  from the unique `id`. Across three worlds (the seed-73 column measured by the gamer-persona
  session):

  | world | land | sites | tier 0 | share | distinct kinds |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | seed 42, size 17 | 2,487 km² | 48 | 38 | 79.2% | 10 |
  | seed 73, size 17 | 5,434 km² | 175 | 148 | 84.6% | 17 |
  | seed 42, size 33 | 3,665 km² | 247 | 131 | 53.0% | 49 |

  **More land at a fixed raster makes it worse (79.2% → 84.6%); more raster at a fixed seed makes
  it better (79.2% → 53.0%).** Adding land without resolution qualifies almost nothing new
  (10 → 17 kinds) and just extends the road network that waystones are strung along, while
  raising the raster admits an octave, the terrain gains variety, and 49 archetypes qualify.
  **Filler share is driven by how many archetypes the terrain can qualify, not by how much
  terrain there is.** State it per-raster; at the sizes 1025 makes available the concentration
  falls, though 53% is still a majority of every place in the world being one line of text.

  Two distinctions worth keeping: **every tier-0 site is a waystone on seed 42 at both rasters
  (38/38, 131/131) but not on seed 73**, whose 148 tier-0 sites are 143 waystones plus 5
  `boundary_stone`. And the **`wayside` family is wider than the `waystone` kind** — 145 against
  131 at size 33, the difference being hedge inns, bridges and ferry crossings, which are tier 1
  and enterable. Any argument about how much is enterable depends on not conflating the two.
- **The story a player is most likely to be offered changes with the grid.** `story_web`'s
  most-offered arc is `the_court` (8 of 54) at size 17 and `the_siege` (42 of 225) at size 33,
  same seed, because tropes read war pressure and threat and those follow settlement count.
  Relayed from the gamer-persona session, not re-measured here.
